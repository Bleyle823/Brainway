Brainway’s positioning uses terms like **sensory-aware**, **neurodivergent-friendly**, and **accessibility-oriented**. Those words describe **design intent** and **software behaviour**, not proven clinical efficacy for any individual learner.

## Evidence and uncertainty

- Many UI and prompt patterns are **informed by** cognitive-multimedia research, cognitive-load framing, and practitioner conversations around **ND-inclusive** teaching. That literature is heterogeneous; effect sizes and populations differ.
- **OpenDyslexic** and similar font interventions have **mixed empirical results**. Treat typography toggles as **optional affordances** and gather your own feedback.
- **Generative video** can introduce **visual artefacts**, **omissions**, or **hallucinated on-screen text** that distort instruction. **Human review** remains necessary for accuracy, alignment with curriculum, and safeguarding.
- Profile presets (**ADHD**, **autism**, **dyslexia**, **sensory**) are **coarse labels**. Real learners are idiosyncratic; combined needs may not be captured by toggles alone.

## What Brainway is not

- **Not a medical device** and not a substitute for professional diagnosis or treatment.
- **Not a replacement** for formal accessibility audits, VPAT/ACR workflows, IEP/504 processes, or institutional policies—especially when minors or vulnerable adults are involved.
- **Not a guarantee** of reduced meltdown risk, seizure risk, or migraine triggers. Generative output can still be surprising; preview content in your context.

## Recommended evaluation practice

When piloting Brainway:

1. **Co-design** with ND learners, families, and accessibility leads—surveys alone are insufficient.
2. **Log consented, anonymised feedback** on specific presets (see roadmap themes in the root README).
3. **Compare outputs** against a rubric: pacing, text density, contrast, audio peaks, factual fidelity.
4. **Disclose** use of AI-generated media where your jurisdiction or institution requires it.
